The init script's part 2 opens with three writes: `0x0818 = 0x7ff`, `0x0810 = 0x1`, `0x0814 =
0xffff` (E006). Nothing else in the boot payload or the DDK names them, the microkernel does not
reach them (E010), and neither the openpvrsgx headers nor Linux's Rogue driver has a register
that fits (E401).

On the laptop `0x0810` implements bit 0 only, `0x0814` bits 16:0 and `0x0818` bits 10:0
(E122). So the init script sets every bit of `0x0810` and `0x0818` and all but the top bit of
`0x0814`. A fill completes exactly with any of them written to 0, to any single bit, or with
any one of bits 0 to 15 of its init value flipped (E122): a single small fill does not depend
on them.

The values still read as a one-bit enable left on (`0x0810`) and two limits or masks left at
or near their widest (`0x0814`, `0x0818`) [infer]. Tried in 11.15, below: a copy and a triangle
with each register narrowed, and loads up to 4096 tiles, with the job's time recorded as well as
its output. What remains is under "Still open".

Narrowed in 11.14 (E401, no run): no public source names these registers. No SGX register header
of the openpvrsgx archive (161 sgx*defs.h files for cores 520 to 545, DDK 1.5 to 1.17, at the pin
E401 names) defines a register from 0x0780 to 0x0880. Linux's Rogue driver has registers at 0x0810
and 0x0818, ROGUE_CR_MIPS_WRAPPER_CONFIG and ROGUE_CR_MIPS_ADDR_REMAP1_CONFIG1, but they are
64-bit registers on an 8-byte stride, with fields that do not match the bits SGX545 implements
(E122), so they do not explain these. That step had no run; the runs are the next paragraphs.

Narrowed in 11.15 (E403, E404, run on the laptop with the user present): with each register
narrowed, down to 0 and all three at once, a copy and a triangle (147 cases each, E122's values),
loads up to a 2048x2048 triangle (4096 tiles), eight back-to-back renders, 64 chained transfers
(no job time is printed for these) and pixel loops (15 cases, 12 workloads, three runs each, 542
runs), no job's median time moves beyond its spread (0.95 to 1.04 of the init values' median
above 1 ms; 0.81 to 1.21 for the two sub-millisecond jobs) and none faults or hangs; one reset
ran the init script again and restored the three. One effect: with `0x0818` holding bit 8
and not bit 10 (`0x100`, `0x1ff`, `0x3ff` of 32 values tried), a triangle drew wrong, the job
reporting ok, when the render before it ran under the same value with a target of another size
(40 of 40 such pairs, two repeats; none with the init value, with a write between the jobs, or
for a copy or fill). So `0x0818` reaches the render path, in a way tied to state kept from one
render to the next [infer]; `0x0810` and `0x0814` showed no effect at any load tried.

Run again with the kernel log kept (E404, E405): the single-job scan (1078 runs: sizes 32 to 512,
every fourth value of `0x0818` and every value with bit 8 set and bit 10 clear, the single bits)
drew exactly in every run, and the pair test over bits 10:8 in all eight combinations with the low
bytes 0x00, 0x7f and 0xff (24 values, both orders of 96 and 128, three repeats, 144 runs) draws the
second render wrong for exactly the six values with bit 8 set and bit 10 clear (bit 9 either), with
the same wrong-pixel count in every repeat; the other 18 draw exactly. So the condition is bit 8 set
with bit 10 clear.

Still open: what bit 8 and bit 10 do (the render state kept from one render to the next, [infer]);
other low bytes (three of 256 tried); pairs with equal sizes and moved geometry; whether `0x0814`
or `0x0810` matter at a load the tool cannot build (a larger parameter buffer, more tasks than a
loop of one program, several contexts at once). Status: narrowed, not answered.
